    How Online Weight Loss Clinics Work, From Questionnaire to Doorstep

    Oakwell MD care team · August 22, 2026 · 6 min read

    An online weight loss clinic replaces the waiting room with a questionnaire and a secure message thread. The medical decisions are the same ones an in-person clinic makes: is a medication appropriate, which one, at what dose. What changes is where it happens. Here is what the process looks like from the first click to the box at your door.

    The process, step by step

    Step one: the health questionnaire

    The intake usually takes 10 to 15 minutes. You will be asked about your weight history, medicines you take now and any drug allergies. Some questions screen for safety, such as any personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease or eating disorders, any of which can change the plan. Pregnancy plans matter too. The Wegovy label advises stopping at least two months before a planned pregnancy.

    Answer honestly, even about things that feel unrelated. Details like an old gallbladder surgery or a history of disordered eating shape the provider's decision more than most people expect. You will also upload a photo ID. Some clinics ask for a full-length photo or a picture of your scale reading to confirm your current weight before the provider signs off.

    Step two: provider review and labs

    A licensed provider in your state reads your answers. They may ask for a photo ID, a recent weight on a scale, or lab results from the past year, such as an A1c and a metabolic panel. Labs can often be drawn at a local lab if you do not have recent results. Then the provider decides. Not everyone qualifies, and a good clinic will tell you so instead of finding a workaround.

    Eligibility for FDA-approved weight loss drugs generally means a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure or sleep apnea. The provider will also ask about medications that interact, such as insulin or sulfonylureas, which raise the risk of low blood sugar. Expect follow-up questions.

    Step three: prescription and pharmacy

    If a medication is appropriate, the provider sends a prescription to a licensed US pharmacy. Brand pens can be filled at a retail pharmacy through insurance, and compounded versions come from a licensed compounding pharmacy. Compounded products are not FDA-approved. Cold-chain shipping, an insulated box with ice packs, keeps refrigerated medication in range. Put it in the fridge the day it arrives.

    Shipping usually takes two to five business days after the pharmacy fills the order. Watch for the tracking email, since cold-packed boxes should not sit on a porch overnight. Brand pens can come with needles included. Vials usually arrive with insulin syringes and prep pads.

    Step four: ongoing check-ins

    The prescription is the start, not the end. Check-ins usually line up with dose changes, about every four weeks while you titrate. Your provider asks about nausea, bowel habits, your weight trend and how much protein you are getting, then decides whether to step up, hold or lower the dose. Refills depend on those answers.

    Messages between check-ins are part of the service. If you have new symptoms, such as vomiting that lasts more than a day or severe belly pain, message right away or seek urgent care. Weight stalls are common around month three. Your provider may adjust the dose or look at protein and sleep before changing anything.

    What a good online clinic does

    • Uses licensed providers who practice in your state.
    • Names the pharmacy filling your prescription and whether the product is FDA-approved.
    • Asks for labs or records when your history calls for them.
    • Makes it easy to message a provider about side effects between visits.
    • Shows pricing clearly after the provider review, without surprise charges.

    Questions patients ask

    Is a video visit required? It depends on your state. A few states call for a real-time visit before prescribing, and many allow an asynchronous review instead. Your clinic should tell you which applies.

    Can you use insurance? Coverage for brand pens depends on your plan, and most insurers want a prior authorization first. Compounded medication is almost always cash-pay. Pricing is shown after the provider review, and it depends on the treatment plan, the medication form and how often you need refills.
